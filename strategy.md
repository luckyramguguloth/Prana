## 1. The Winning Thesis

Every City Battle scores the same six things. Nothing else matters. Read the rubric like a scorer, not a builder:

| # | Dimension | Weight | Who scores it | What actually earns points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | End product quality | 30% | Jury | Works reliably live, solves a real problem, feels finish-able into a real product |
| 2 | Novelty & impact | 20% | Jury | An angle jurors haven't seen five times that day, tied to real-world impact |
| 3 | Creative phone use | 15% | HackTracker (device data) | Camera **and** voice **and** on-device AI, all genuinely load-bearing, not bolted on |
| 4 | Technical depth | 15% | Jury | Architecture, code quality, robustness, real hardware use (NPU, not just an API call) |
| 5 | Office Kit usage | 10% | HackTracker (device data) | Real Red-Light development flow, not five minutes of screen mirroring for show |
| 6 | Demo & presentation | 10% | Jury | A tight, rehearsed 3–5 min live pitch on the phone |

**The strategic insight:** 55% of hackathon teams will build a wrapper around a cloud LLM API and call it "AI-powered." That gets crushed on dimension 3 (no real on-device AI → no HackTracker credit) and dimension 4 (no real hardware use → shallow technical depth). The jury has explicitly been told "a well-built simple product beats a broken complex one" — so the winning move is not the most ambitious idea, it's the **narrowest idea that is fully, verifiably on-device**, built with production-grade engineering discipline, demoed flawlessly.

Two structural notes reinforce this:
- HealthTech is a **city-battle-only** track (it doesn't carry to the Grand Finale directly), so within Hyderabad this is scored purely against other HealthTech teams — you don't need to out-build a fintech or dev-tools team, just the best HealthTech room.
- Hyderabad's own city description calls out **"enterprise scale and health-data depth."** The jury here will specifically expect data-privacy maturity in a HealthTech build — this is a scoring lever most teams will ignore. Make it visible.

## 2. What We Build: **"Prana"** — an on-device, multimodal wellness check-in

> *A 90-second daily check-in that reads your heart rate from the camera and your stress/mood from your voice — entirely on the phone, with zero data ever leaving the device.*

### 2.1 The core loop (what a user actually does)
1. Open the app. Tap **"Check in."**
2. **30s camera step:** place fingertip over the rear camera + flash (or look at the front camera). The app extracts heart rate and heart-rate variability (HRV) via remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) run **entirely on-device**.
3. **20s voice step:** speak one prompted sentence ("Tell me how your day's been"). An on-device speech model extracts prosodic/emotion features (stress, fatigue, valence) — reusing the same CNN‑BiLSTM speech-emotion approach already validated in prior work (91%+ accuracy class of model, shrunk to a mobile-sized network).
4. **On-device fusion + local small language model** turns {HR, HRV, voice-stress score, recent trend} into one paragraph of plain-language, non-diagnostic guidance ("Your HRV is a bit low today and your voice reads more tense than your weekly average — a slow-breathing break before your next meeting may help") — generated by a small open-source on-device LLM (see §5.3), not a template string, so it visibly reasons over real fused signals.
5. A **7‑day trend view** (line chart, on-device SQLite) shows the user this is worth returning to daily — this is what answers "would someone keep using it."
6. Everything computed and stored stays on-device. A one-tap **"Export as encrypted PDF"** (for sharing with a doctor) is the only outbound path, and it's user-initiated, encrypted, and logged.

### 2.2 Why this specific idea wins each dimension

- **End product quality (30%):** a complete, working, closed-loop product in a single sitting — check-in → insight → trend → optional export. No half-built screens. Deliberately narrow scope, deliberately polished.
- **Novelty & impact (20%):** multimodal (camera + voice) *fused* on-device wellness scoring is not the generic "AI chatbot for your symptoms" every other HealthTech team will pitch. The privacy angle (no cloud, ever) is a genuine differentiator jurors can articulate afterward.
- **Creative phone use (15%, HackTracker):** camera used for real signal extraction (not just photo capture), microphone used for a real ML task, and on-device AI (rPPG model + speech model + local LLM) run natively. This is the single highest-leverage dimension to max out, and this idea is built to max it.
- **Technical depth (15%):** real signal processing (rPPG is a genuinely hard, respected CV problem — FFT/bandpass filtering on a noisy face/finger video), a real edge ML pipeline, on-device NPU delegate usage, clean layered architecture, tests. This is where jurors distinguish "vibe-coded" from engineered.
- **Office Kit usage (10%, HackTracker):** the whole build is Android-native, so the natural Red-Light workflow (editing code on the mirrored laptop screen, moving log files/model files back and forth via Office Kit file transfer, copy-pasting stack traces via shared clipboard) is authentic, not staged. §9 gives you a script to make sure this actually happens and gets captured.
- **Demo & presentation (10%):** a live check-in in front of the jury is inherently visual and dramatic — heart rate ticking up on screen, a spoken sentence, an insight appearing in under 3 seconds. This is a demo that *shows itself* rather than needing narration.

### 2.3 Naming
Primary: **Prana** (Sanskrit for "life force / breath" — one word, works in English and Telugu/Hindi contexts, easy to say in a 3-minute pitch). Alternatives if you want to test with mentors: **Nabhi**, **VitalSense**, **PulseKeeper**. Pick one and commit before Saturday — do not rename mid-build.

### 2.4 Explicit non-goals (say these out loud to the jury — scope discipline itself signals maturity)
- This is **not** a diagnostic device and never claims to detect a medical condition. It is a *wellness awareness* tool. State this clearly in-app and in the pitch — it pre-empts the hardest jury question and shows domain maturity.
- No cloud inference, no user accounts, no login wall for the MVP — friction kills "would someone keep using it."
- No multi-user, no social features, no gamification beyond the streak — cut anything that isn't on the critical path before Saturday night.

**Zero network calls in the release build.** This is a deliberate, provable architectural claim — add a build-time check (see §7) that fails CI if any networking permission or HTTP client dependency sneaks into the health-data path. This single fact is your strongest "technical depth" + "novelty" talking point.

### 3.1 Why native Android (not Flutter/React Native/PWA)
The rubric explicitly rewards "real use of the hardware" and NPU delegate access (QNN/NNAPI) is most mature and lowest-latency through native Android + LiteRT. Native also gives full-fidelity Camera2/CameraX access for rPPG (frame-level control over exposure/frame rate matters for signal quality) and low-latency `AudioRecord` access for voice capture. If, and only if, you are already fluent in Flutter and not in Kotlin, Flutter with platform channels to a native TFLite delegate is an acceptable fallback — but budget extra hours for the channel plumbing.

## 4. Model & Data Strategy

### 4.1 rPPG (camera → heart rate/HRV)
- **Do not train a CNN from scratch in 30 hours.** Use a known, published, lightweight rPPG approach: green-channel or chrominance-based (CHROM/POS algorithm) signal extraction from face or fingertip video, followed by bandpass filtering (0.7–4 Hz) and FFT peak detection for BPM, then RMSSD for a simple HRV proxy. This is classical signal processing, not deep learning — it is fast to implement, explainable to a jury ("here is the actual waveform we extract"), and genuinely robust in 30 hours. Wrap it as `RppgSignalProcessor`.
- Optionally layer a tiny on-device CNN (MobileNet-style, <1MB, quantized) on top purely for **motion-artifact rejection** (classify "usable frame" vs "too much motion/lighting") — this is enough to legitimately claim on-device *deep learning* in the camera path if you have time, without betting the whole feature on it.
- Calibrate against a known reference on your own device before demo day (e.g., compare against a fitness band or manual pulse count) and note accuracy honestly in the README ("±X BPM vs manual reference, tested on N=3 sessions"). Honest, measured numbers score far better with a technical jury than an unverified claim.

### 4.2 Voice → stress/emotion
- Reuse the CNN-BiLSTM speech-emotion architecture pattern already validated in prior work, but **distill/shrink it** for mobile: fewer BiLSTM units, fewer conv filters, INT8 post-training quantization via TFLite converter. Target model size <10MB, inference <300ms on-device.
- If there's no time to retrain, use a small pretrained open-source speech-emotion model (e.g., a distilled Wav2Vec2/HuBERT emotion classifier or a lightweight MFCC+CNN model from an open dataset like RAVDESS/CREMA-D) converted to TFLite. Attribute the dataset/base model explicitly in the README (hackathon rules require attribution for open-source components).
- Output should be a simple 3-class or continuous score (calm / neutral / stressed) — resist the urge to output fine-grained emotion wheels; a jury cares about "does the number mean something," not granularity.

### 4.3 On-device LLM for the insight paragraph
- Use a small open-source instruction-tuned model runnable fully offline via **MediaPipe LLM Inference API** or **LiteRT-LM**: candidates are **Gemma 3 270M/1B-IT** or **Llama 3.2 1B-Instruct** (quantized, `.task`/`.tflite` bundle). This directly satisfies the hackathon's explicit "local or open-source model at the core earns brownie points" nudge, in text.
- Prompt template (deterministic, short, guardrailed):
  ```
  You are a calm, non-diagnostic wellness coach. Given these on-device measurements,
  write ONE short paragraph (max 3 sentences) of plain-language, encouraging,
  non-medical guidance. Never mention diseases or diagnoses.

  Today: HR={hr} bpm, HRV={hrv} ms, voice_stress={stress}/10.
  7-day average: HR={avg_hr}, HRV={avg_hrv}, voice_stress={avg_stress}.
  ```
- Add a **content filter/guardrail function** that scans the model output for disallowed medical-claim keywords ("diagnos", "disease", "cancer", "prescri") and falls back to a safe templated sentence if triggered. This is a small, visible piece of engineering that scores strongly on both technical depth and "data quality/safety" — mention it explicitly in the demo.

### 4.4 Data quality practices to state explicitly in the README
- Document exact sample size / conditions the rPPG and voice models were validated under, and the known failure modes (e.g., "requires steady 10s hold, works best in indoor lighting 300–600 lux"; "voice model trained on adult speech, English/Hindi-accented English; may misclassify heavy background noise as stress").
- Ship a small **synthetic/adversarial test set** (a folder of edge-case audio/video clips: silence, very noisy audio, no finger detected, extreme motion) and a script that runs the pipeline against them and reports pass/fail — this is your "data quality" evidence artifact for the jury.

## 5. Security, Privacy & Cryptography (this is a scoring lever, not paperwork)

Because HealthTech + "health-data depth" is explicitly called out for Hyderabad, treat this section as differentiating content, not boilerplate.

1. **On-device only, provably.** No `INTERNET` permission in the release manifest for the health-data path. Add a CI check (`scripts/check_no_network.sh`) that greps the manifest and dependency graph and fails the build if a networking library appears outside an explicitly whitelisted "export" module.
2. **Encryption at rest.** Room database encrypted with **SQLCipher**; the SQLCipher passphrase itself is generated once and stored via the **Android Keystore** (hardware-backed on Snapdragon devices) — never stored in plaintext, never hardcoded.
3. **Encryption in transit (the one deliberate exception).** The optional "export as PDF for your doctor" feature: generate the PDF locally, then encrypt it with a **user-chosen passcode using AES-256-GCM** before it can be shared via Android's share sheet. Show the encryption happening in the demo — it's a strong, concrete, few-second beat that visually proves "we thought about privacy."
4. **Least-privilege permissions.** Request `CAMERA` and `RECORD_AUDIO` only, request them just-in-time (not at launch), with a plain-language rationale screen before the OS prompt.
5. **Data minimization & right-to-delete.** A visible "Delete all my data" button in Settings that wipes the encrypted DB immediately, with a confirmation dialog. State in the README how this maps to India's **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** principles (purpose limitation, data minimization, right to erasure) — this single paragraph signals real domain awareness to a jury that includes an Enterprise Security lead from Microsoft.
6. **No third-party analytics/crash SDKs** wired to the health-data screens (fine to keep a local-only crash log file).
7. **Threat model doc.** Add `docs/THREAT_MODEL.md` — one page: assets (biometric data, voice recordings), actors (curious bystander, lost/stolen phone, malicious app on same device), mitigations mapped to each (screen lock requirement, Keystore-backed encryption, no clipboard leakage of raw data, no background mic access). This one file is disproportionately persuasive to a technical jury — most hackathon teams have none.

## 6. Testing Strategy

Don't over-invest — 30 hours is short — but a *visible, running* test suite is one of the cheapest technical-depth points available.

- **Unit tests (JUnit + Kotlin):**
  - `RppgSignalProcessorTest` — feed a synthetic sine-wave-based "video signal" at a known frequency, assert recovered BPM is within tolerance.
  - `WellnessFusionEngineTest` — table-driven tests over {HR, HRV, stress} → expected `WellnessState` bucket.
  - `InsightGuardrailTest` — feed known-bad model outputs, assert the guardrail catches them and falls back safely.
- **Instrumented/UI tests (Compose testing + Espresso):** one smoke test that walks CheckIn → Insight → Trend without crashing.
- **Model regression tests:** a small script (`tools/eval_models.py`) that runs the quantized TFLite models against the edge-case clips from §4.4 and asserts no crash + output within expected numeric range.
- **CI gate:** all of the above run on every push via GitHub Actions (§8) — a green badge in the README is worth more to a jury in 10 seconds than a paragraph explaining your testing philosophy.

## 8. GitHub & CI/CD

- **Repo hygiene:** small, frequent, well-labeled commits from hour 1 (`feat:`, `fix:`, `test:`, `docs:` conventional commit prefixes). A commit history that visibly builds up over the 30 hours *is itself evidence* the build happened inside the event window, addressing the "original work only" rule directly if organizers spot-check.
- **Branches:** `main` always deployable; short-lived feature branches (`feat/rppg`, `feat/voice-model`, `feat/insight-llm`) merged via PRs even solo — PRs give you a changelog and a place to note design decisions.
- **`.github/workflows/android-ci.yml`:** on every push — `./gradlew testDebugUnitTest`, `./gradlew lint`, `./gradlew assembleDebug`. Fail fast, badge in README.
- **`.github/workflows/privacy-gate.yml`:** runs `tools/check_no_network.sh` and fails the build if any networking dependency/permission is detected outside the whitelisted export module. This workflow existing at all, named plainly, is a strong signal to any judge who opens the Actions tab.
- **Issues board:** keep a lightweight GitHub Projects board (`Backlog / In Progress / Done`) — a screenshot of this in the pitch deck is a fast, credible "we run this like a real product team" beat.

## 9. Office Kit & HackTracker Optimization (10% + 15% of the grade — don't leave this to chance)

HackTracker scores **counts and durations**, not intentions. Make usage real and frequent, not staged:

- During every **Red Light** block, actually develop through Office Kit: mirror the laptop screen onto the phone if convenient for reviewing code while away from the desk, but more importantly use **file transfer** to move exported model files, log files, and screenshots between phone and laptop as part of the real workflow (e.g., pull a crash log off the phone via Office Kit file transfer instead of email/USB), and use **shared clipboard** to move stack traces, API prompts, and commit messages between devices.
- Do **not** try to "fake" usage by opening Office Kit and idling — HackTracker measures genuine interaction; the honest workflow above naturally produces strong duration/interaction numbers because Red Light literally has no other route to the laptop.
- For "creative phone use," make sure the **camera and microphone are exercised heavily during testing**, not just once for the final demo — every rPPG calibration run and every voice-model test clip you record on-device between hours 5 and 25 is itself scored telemetry.
- Assign yourself (mentally, since solo) explicit checkpoints every ~4 hours to touch camera, mic, and Office Kit at least once, so usage is spread across the whole window rather than clustered right before Eval Round 1/2 — spread usage reads as authentic building, not last-minute gaming.

## 10. UI/UX Design Direction (avoid "AI slop")

Ground every choice in the actual subject matter — this is a calm, clinical-but-warm wellness tool, not a generic SaaS dashboard and not a flashy gradient app.

- **Palette:** move away from default AI-generated palettes (no warm-cream+terracotta, no near-black+neon-green, no default Material purple). Ground it in the product: a **muted sage/teal base (`#3E5C50`)**, a warm off-white background (`#F7F5F0` — not `#F4F1EA`, keep it slightly warmer/greyer to avoid the generic tell), a single deliberate accent for "attention" states like elevated stress (`#C1553D`, a muted brick, not a saturated alert-red), and near-black text at `#1C1B19` rather than pure `#000` or `#0B0B0B`.
- **Typography:** one humanist sans for everything (e.g., **Inter** or **Manrope**) at a clear type scale (13/15/17/22/32sp), no all-caps labels, no tracked-out eyebrow text above every section. Use weight and size, not color, to carry hierarchy.
- **The hero moment:** the CheckIn screen's live camera preview with a real-time pulsing waveform overlay (the actual extracted rPPG signal, not a decorative animation) *is* the hero — it's the single most memorable, on-brand visual beat in the whole app. Spend your design effort there; keep every other screen quiet and disciplined.
- **Motion:** one deliberate animation — the wellness score gently resolving into place as the fusion completes (a few hundred ms), and the waveform drawing live during capture. No fade-and-slide-up on every card, no hover effects (this is a phone app — no hover state exists anyway; watch for template Compose code that assumes desktop patterns).
- **Copy voice:** plain, second-person, non-clinical, never diagnostic ("Your voice sounds a little more tense than usual today" not "Elevated cortisol markers detected"). Errors explain what happened and what to do ("Couldn't get a clear pulse — hold your finger steady and try again" not "Signal acquisition failed").
- **Accessibility floor:** minimum 4.5:1 text contrast, visible focus states for TalkBack, camera/mic permission rationale screens readable at default font size, works down to a standard 6.1" phone viewport (it will — you're demoing on the loaner iQOO device itself).

## 13. Live Demo Script (target: 4 minutes, rehearsed until boring)

1. **(0:00–0:20)** "Prana is a 90-second daily health check-in that reads your heart rate from the camera and your stress from your voice — and none of it ever leaves this phone." *(Open app on the iQOO device, ideally mirrored via Office Kit onto a screen the jury can see.)*

## 15. Judging-Day Playbook

- **Anticipate the hardest question first:** *"How is this different from a fitness tracker / an existing wellness app?"* Answer prepared: on-device multimodal fusion (most trackers are single-signal and cloud-backed), zero-cloud privacy guarantee, and a generative — not templated — insight layer, all running on hardware the jury is literally holding.
- **Anticipate the medical-claims question:** *"Is this medically validated?"* Answer prepared, calmly: "No — it's explicitly a wellness-awareness tool, not a diagnostic device, and the app states that directly to the user. We designed the guardrails specifically to prevent it from ever making a medical claim." This answer, delivered confidently, often scores *better* than overclaiming would.
- **Bring the mentors in early**, specifically ones with Android/Qualcomm/security backgrounds — a mentor who has seen your NPU delegate code before the jury does is more likely to speak well of you informally, and their feedback will harden your Eval Round 1 build.
- **Don't oversell in the pitch.** State the honest validation numbers from §4. Jurors evaluating "technical depth" specifically distrust unqualified claims of accuracy.

## 16. Risk Register

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| rPPG signal too noisy live on stage | Practice the exact hand position/lighting beforehand; have a fallback "best of 3 attempts" flow rather than one shot; pre-seed trend data so a single noisy live reading doesn't sink the whole demo |
| On-device LLM too slow/large for the loaner phone's RAM | Default to the smallest model tier (Gemma 3 270M) and test on a comparable mid-range Snapdragon device before event day if at all possible; have a lightweight rules-based fallback insight generator behind the same interface so the demo never visibly fails |
| Solo builder burnout at hour 20 | The timeline in §14 has a real overnight rest window built in — use it; a scoped MVP delivered calmly beats an ambitious build delivered exhausted |
| Scope creep (adding features Saturday night) | §2.4's non-goals list exists precisely to be pointed back to under pressure — do not add features after Eval Round 1 |
| CI/network-gate false failure blocking a late push | Keep the privacy-gate workflow simple and test it early (hour 2–3), not at hour 28 |
| Camera/mic permission denial during live demo | Pre-grant permissions well before your pitch slot; never demo the permission-request flow live unless specifically asked |

## 18. Solo-Builder Workflow with an AI Coding Assistant

Since you intend to hand this document to an AI assistant to build from:
1. Paste **§7 (Repository Structure)** first and have it scaffold the full skeleton with empty/stub files and the two GitHub Actions workflows before writing any feature logic — this guarantees CI is green from commit one.
